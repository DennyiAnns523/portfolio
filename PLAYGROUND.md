# Dennyi's continuous world

As you scroll, the hero is pulled toward the centre of the screen and swallowed by a star field and a black-hole accretion disk; the passage text follows it in, and the court opens out of the dark in a widening circle. One object then carries you through the scenes: the shadow at the black hole's heart shrinks into the basketball on the free-throw line, the ball lifts off and folds into the Rubik's cube (drawn from its current, possibly scrambled, state), and the cube breaks into stickers that tumble into the garden pot as seeds. Scrolling back plays each one in reverse. The court, cube, and garden are full-screen scenes with blending backgrounds and no enclosing cards. Each scene snaps to fill the screen, so a small scroll settles back into it, and a scene stays whole and playable until it is 40% scrolled away; only then does the ball lift off or the cube come apart. This is the home page: Next.js serves `public/world-demo.html` at `/`.

- **Hero island:** a mini me wanders a wide floating island with a pond, grass, a lantern that glows after hours, butterflies, drifting clouds and three distant islets. It walks around the tree, portal, pedestal and pond, and turns to face you when it stops. Moving the pointer tilts the island and steers its gaze. Tap it to make it jump and wave (its greeting changes each time), tap an orbiting orb to pop it (it grows back), or tap the ground to plant a flower, which it walks over to see. Headline letters lift and turn orange near the pointer. The island sizes itself to the space the text leaves: beside the headline on laptops and monitors, below it on phones. It re-fits on resize and when the window moves to a screen with a different pixel ratio.
- **Mini me:** a stylised blocky figure based on Dennyi's photos: a short black bob with bangs, black rectangular glasses, the pink patterned bandana with a knot at the back, a white tee with a crossbody strap, black wide-leg track pants with white side stripes, and cream clogs. It is built from boxes and spheres, not scanned from a photo. When the cat appears it calls out 'Hi Moriarrr!', and the cat answers.
- **Black hole:** the camera leans with the pointer, so the disk tilts and the light bends. Tap anywhere in the passage to throw in a star; it spirals down, stretches, reddens and flashes the photon ring when swallowed, and a counter keeps score. Dust drifts off the pointer, stray stars fall in on their own, and scrolling spins the disk faster.
- **Free throws:** an arena seen from behind mini me, who holds the ball for a set shot. Painted key, free-throw circle and three-point arc, a stanchion on the baseline, sweeping spotlights, a jumbotron and a crowd that sways, does a wave on a basket and slumps on a miss. The camera leans with the pointer. After every shot mini me dribbles to the next of eight spots around the key, in a fixed tour; the current spot glows on the floor. Aim is relative to the hoop, so a straight drag goes at the rim from any spot; only the power changes. Drag upward from the ball and release to shoot. Horizontal movement changes aim; drag length changes power. Drag the backboard sideways to reposition it; −/+ changes distance. The whole court and controls fit in the viewport. Focus the canvas for arrow-key aim/power and Space to shoot, or use the Shoot button. Focus the backboard and use left/right arrows to move it.
- **Scoring:** the same 3D trajectory is simulated and drawn. Gravity, rim/backboard collisions, ball clearance through the opening, and a downward passage determine the score. The ball is never snapped into the net.
- **Rubik's cube:** a large cube centred under a short title, with the face turns and tools in a compact row beneath it. Legal 3×3 layer turns, inverse turns, undo, scrambling, and a solved reset. Drag to orbit. With the canvas focused, use arrow keys to orbit and F/R/U/L/D/B to turn faces; Shift reverses a turn.
- **Garden:** a real-time 3D scene (WebGL, Three.js) seen from standing height: a cedar raised bed of dark, crumbly soil in a lawn of wind-blown grass blades, with a picket fence, a hedge and trees fading into haze, under a physically based sky. Lighting is ACES tone-mapped with soft sun shadows and sky reflections. Pick a seed from the dock (tomato, sunflower or strawberry) and tap the soil; the plant grows from real geometry (tapered stems, veined and serrated leaves, flowers, glossy fruit) as it is watered, and more slowly in low light. Drag across the bed to water with a galvanised can that pours a stream; wet soil darkens and slowly dries. Tomatoes and strawberries flower, set green fruit and ripen to red; tap ripe fruit and it lifts into the basket, and the plant fruits again after more water. Sunflowers turn their heads to the sun and bow at dusk; a ripe head is harvested whole. The time-of-day slider moves the sun on a real arc (rising right, noon behind you, setting left), changing light colour, shadow length and fog; after 20:00 night falls with moonlight and fireflies. Rain greys the sky and waters everything. Bees visit open flowers and a monarch butterfly drifts by. With the garden focused, left and right arrows move a marker along the bed and Enter plants, waters or picks there. Slow GPUs drop shadows and grass density, and the resolution adapts if frames run slow. Without WebGL the flat canvas garden (`garden-life.js`) takes over. All textures are drawn in code; there are no image files. The harvest count persists locally; the garden restarts on reload with three plants.
- **Cat:** a fat black animated sprite walks, rolls, or chases yarn across the current scene at random intervals. The roll is choreographed from the walk sprite: the cat crouches, tips over onto its back, wriggles belly-up with its paws paddling, carries on over onto its feet and pops up, with dust puffs, and its body curls rounder and lifts so it stays on the floor as it turns. "Invite a garden cat" asks for a roll. Clicking or tapping it shows a short speech bubble that changes with the current scene, so it takes clicks only where it is standing. Drag it to pick it up by the scruff: it dangles and swings, and when you let go it flies, bounces, lands and carries on walking. Stroking it back and forth with the mouse makes it purr and float hearts. It notices a basket, a solved cube and a harvest, and a miss brings it out to mock you (at most every 15 seconds), with different jokes for airballs, rim-outs and bricks off the glass; if it is not already visiting it wanders over (at most every 40 seconds) to comment. It never opens a modal, navigates, or takes focus. `pspsps…` summons it; the cat-visits toggle disables automatic visits; Escape ends a visit. Background tabs and active shots defer automatic visits. This is an AI-generated sprite animation, not footage or a rigged 3D animal.
- **Gentle motion:** respects reduced-motion preferences, stops ambient cat visits, simplifies the passage, disables the ball surprise, pauses the hero, and resolves shots without flight animation.

The black-hole shader is a cinematic light-bending approximation, not a scientifically calibrated simulation. A canvas fallback works without WebGL, and software renderers use lower resolution and cadence.

Dennyi's photos in `my3dmodel/` were used only as reference for the mini me's look; they are not served or copied into `public/`. No deployment or GitHub push has been performed.

## Files

`public/world-demo.html` is the page markup and styles; `public/world.js` runs the hero island and the doors (including "Say hello" and the shared idea garden, which call `/api/contact` and `/api/ideas`), and `public/doors.css` styles those forms. `public/garden-loader.js` loads the 3D garden module and falls back to the flat garden if it fails. The page has no inline scripts apart from the import map, so it runs under a strict Content-Security-Policy; keep new code in files. `public/experiments.js` handles content, the cube, and the garden. `public/immersive.js` renders the black hole and perspective basketball. `public/world-flow.js` handles scene transitions and the court reveal. `public/cat-companion.js` drives the ambient cat and its speech bubbles. `public/scene-bridges.js` draws the ball, cube and seed transitions on one overlay canvas. `gravity-flow.css` styles the pull into the black hole and the cat's handling. `public/garden-3d.js` is the 3D garden (an ES module using `public/vendor/three/`, Three.js r170, MIT licence); `public/garden-life.js` is its flat fallback. `public/scenes.css` lays out the cube and garden scenes. `public/fit.css` holds the layout fixes that keep scenes, controls and the floating dock apart on small phones, landscape phones, portrait tablets and short laptop screens. `public/games-core.js` contains deterministic game rules. Corresponding CSS files style these layers.

`public/media/black-cat-walk.png` is the transparent eight-frame cat sprite. `public/media/GENERATED-ASSETS.md` records the generation method and exact prompt.

Run `node preview.cjs` from the workspace root and open http://127.0.0.1:4173, or serve the `public` directory with any local HTTP server and visit `/world-demo.html`. `npm run dev` serves the same page at `/` together with the API; the static preview has no API, so "Say hello" and idea sharing report a failure there.

## Rules verification

```sh
node --test tests/games-core.test.cjs
```

Every one of the eight shooting spots can score with a straight drag (power roughly 35–80%). Tests verify cube identities, mixed move reversal, adjacent stickers, ball clearance, moved hoops, collisions, and trajectory continuity. The workspace `check-world.cjs` checks the scenes and gestures in a local headless Chrome browser at desktop and mobile sizes.
